Overall Output

Peak ≠ Most Efficient

February 2026 had the highest headcount (137.4 people/day) and highest output (30,212 units) — but only 220 units per person per day. March dropped to 96 HC (−30%) and produced 252 u/person, the best efficiency on record. More people did not produce proportionally more.
Headcount Trend

As headcount normalized from 137 (Feb) to 80 (May), production held comparatively well: Apr–May average 19,928 units at 241 u/person/day. The team is operating more efficiently at smaller size — Oct 2025 had 127 HC but only 174 u/person, the lowest efficiency in the dataset.
Optimal Range
85–100 HC delivers the best sustained efficiency — 241–252 u/person/day
Mar–May 2026 (96→85→80 HC) consistently produce 241–252 units per person per day — better than any higher-headcount month. The current run rate of ~80 people producing ~19k units/month appears to be the stable operational floor, not a degraded state.

Combined Output
37,370 units processed — screen swaps (60%) and repairs (40%) running in parallel
The OpenCell department delivered 22,297 new TVs from screen swaps and 15,073 DMT repairs Jul 2025–May 2026. Both sub-operations ran simultaneously, each feeding separate output streams — new inventory from swaps, repaired units from DMT.
Screen Swap Peak
March 2026 — 4,439 new TVs, the only month above 4,000 and 2× the active average
Excluding January 2026 (lack of screens), the 10-month average is 2,215 swaps/month. March hit 4,439 — a concentrated production push. No comparable spike in adjacent months suggests a deliberate batch run, not a sustained ramp.
Repair Acceleration
DMT repairs tripled — from ~900/month in Q3 2025 to 3,068 in May 2026
Average monthly repairs in Jul–Sep 2025 was 894 units. By April–May 2026 the operation reached 2,221 and 3,068 respectively — the highest months on record. May 2026 already surpasses every prior month with the month still open.
Independent Supply Chains
January 2026 — swaps dropped to 151 units, repairs held at 934 — two ops, two inputs
The January screen shortage brought swaps to near-zero without affecting DMT. Repairs depend on incoming broken TVs; swaps depend on screen inventory. A disruption in one does not cascade to the other — an operational resilience worth preserving.
Brand Concentration
Onn + Vizio = 58.7% of DMT repairs — Samsung, TCL, Hisense complete the Top 5
Of 15,073 total repairs, Onn accounts for 5,070 (33.6%) and Vizio for 3,833 (25.4%). Samsung (2,228), TCL (1,930), and Hisense (830) are next. The top 5 brands represent 91.8% of all repair volume — planning capacity around them covers the vast majority of throughput.

Headcount by Activity — How the Dept. Deploys Its People

Headcount Insight

Activity Shift
OpenCell dominated Oct–Dec 2025; DMT took over from Jan 2026 and peaked at 246 in May
The dept started as primarily a screen-swap operation (OpenCell = 60–70% of HC in Oct–Dec 2025). From January 2026, DMT repairs became the primary headcount absorber. By May 2026, DMT (246) represents 59% of the total department headcount — a structural rebalancing, not a temporary spike.
New Activities
Kits (Mar 2026) and Versiones (Feb 2026) signal operational expansion — not just volume growth
Kit separation (Kits/OC) appeared in March 2026 and grew to 129 person-days in April — the third-largest activity by May. Version capture (Versiones/OC) began in February. These are new structured activities, not ad-hoc assignments, which means the dept's operational footprint is actively expanding.
